                      Where these measurements come from

                      A trend needs readings taken on a timetable, not whenever somebody opens the app. A daily Windows task measures free space even with CleanDrive closed — it reads the drive and writes one line, and deletes nothing.

                                          Developer

                                          Things that are useful if you know exactly what they do, and a nuisance otherwise. Everything here is off until you switch it on.

                                          Adds a third button to the Duplicates screen. It makes the copies you pick into extra names for one file, so the disk holds the contents once. Nothing is deleted and every name keeps working. Editing through one name then changes what all of them show, and deleting one frees nothing until the last one goes. It only works between copies on the same NTFS drive, and it skips documents, files in a sync folder and files the Photos screen manages.

                                          Right-click menu in Explorer

                                          Adds “Analyse with CleanDrive” to folders and drives, and “Find duplicates with CleanDrive” to files. On Windows 11 they are under Show more options (or Shift+F10). They are written for your account only, and taken out again when this is switched off or the app is uninstalled.

                                          Scan history

                                          After every scan the app keeps a compressed outline of the folder — how big each folder in it is, and its largest files — so two scans can later be compared. It names files, so it never leaves this computer. The newest are kept, and one a month.

                                          Move to another drive

                                          Managed by your organisation

                                                Make a smaller copy?

                                                A new file is written next to the original, with .cleandrive.mp4 added to its name. Nothing is deleted and nothing is replaced. The original stays exactly where it is, and putting it in the Recycle Bin afterwards is yours to do, once you have looked at both.

                                                This uses more space, not less. Until you delete an original, the drive is holding both copies of it.

                                                How small

                                                The capture date and the rotation are carried over. The location, if the video had one, is deliberately not: writing it into a new file is the one thing this app refuses to do with a coordinate.

                                                Join these copies into one file?

                                                After this, the copies are one file. Editing one edits them all. Deleting one frees nothing until every one of them is deleted.

                                                Nothing is deleted and nothing moves. Each copy you picked stops being its own file and becomes another name for the copy being kept. Every path goes on working, and every program that opens one sees exactly what it saw before.

                                                There is one way this goes wrong quietly, and it was measured on this computer rather than guessed at. Some programs save a file by writing a new one over the top instead of writing into the one that is there. Word and Excel both do. When that happens the join silently comes apart: the copy you saved keeps your changes, the other names stay at the old contents, and the space comes back without anything saying so. Documents are skipped for that reason — but the app cannot know how every program on your computer saves, so this is a real limit and not a solved problem.

                                                Also skipped: copies on different drives, drives that are not NTFS, anything inside OneDrive or another sync folder, and anything in the folders the Photos screen manages.

                                                You can undo this from the Restore Center, which splits each name back into a file of its own and needs room for a full copy of each. What it restores is their separateness, not their old contents: anything written while they were joined is what every copy will have.
